title: The Fights I Want to Choose
date: 2026-09-15
summary: Some goals have a summit. This one only has the next moment in which I get to practice.
---

I put a lot of tangible things on my vision board. Skills I want to learn. Things I want to build. Places I want to visit. Half Dome, someday.

I like goals like these because they are easy to understand and, eventually, easy to measure. I either learned the skill or I didn't. I built the thing or I didn't. I made it to the top or I didn't. There is usually some point at which I can look back and say, I did that.

But lately, I have been thinking about putting something very different on my vision board.

I want to become better at choosing what deserves a fight from me.

More specifically, I want to fight harder when something is genuinely wrong and fight less simply because my ego has been hurt.

This is a much harder goal to measure.

There is no certificate at the end of it. No photograph from the summit. I can't give myself a score at the end of the year and decide that I have successfully become this person.

The test happens in very ordinary moments.

Someone says something that feels disrespectful. Someone doesn't acknowledge something I did. Someone misunderstands me. Someone dismisses me or makes me feel less important than I think I should be.

My instinct in those moments can be immediate. I want to explain myself. I want the other person to understand why they were wrong. I want to make sure they don't think they can treat me that way. Sometimes I simply want to have the last word.

And I have started noticing how easily I can call all of this standing up for myself.

Sometimes it is.

But sometimes, if I am completely truthful with myself, nothing important is actually at stake. My pride has been hurt, and I want to repair it.

So I have been practicing a question:

> If my ego weren't involved, would I still care this much?

It is an annoyingly effective question.

Sometimes the answer is yes. Something genuinely unfair happened. A boundary was crossed. Someone is being harmed. Staying quiet would allow something that I believe is wrong to continue.

Those are not the moments in which I want to teach myself to be silent.

In fact, I want the opposite.

I want to be willing to speak when speaking is uncomfortable. I want to stand up for someone even when the situation has nothing to do with me. I want to question something that is unfair even when ignoring it would be easier for me. I want to have the courage to become inconvenient when something actually matters.

But surprisingly often, when I ask myself that question, the answer is no.

Nothing meaningful will change if I win this argument. Nobody needs protecting. No principle I deeply care about is being compromised. I simply want another person to acknowledge that I was right, that they were wrong, or that I deserved better.

Those are the fights I am trying to become less available for.

That doesn't mean I think disrespect should always be tolerated. It doesn't mean silence is automatically the mature response. And it certainly doesn't mean I have mastered any of this.

I still replay conversations in my head. I still think of the perfect response several hours after I needed it. I still feel the urge to prove a point that, objectively, stopped mattering a long time ago.

I am just becoming more aware of what that urge is asking me to protect.

And I am beginning to wonder how much energy we spend defending our image of ourselves, energy that could be used for things we actually believe in.

Maybe that is what I want to change.

I want some of the energy I have spent defending myself to become available for defending what matters.

Which brings me back to my vision board.

How do I know, a year from now, whether I have actually grown in this way?

I don't think I can count the number of arguments I avoided. Avoiding conflict isn't necessarily growth. Sometimes walking away is wisdom; sometimes it is fear.

I don't think I can measure it by how calm I remained either. Anger isn't always evidence that I have failed. There are things worth being angry about.

Maybe the only way I can measure this goal is by looking honestly at the moments when I had a choice.

When I stayed quiet, was it because my ego didn't need defending, or because speaking up would have been uncomfortable?

When I spoke, was it because something mattered, or because I desperately needed to win?

Could I allow someone to misunderstand me without feeling compelled to correct their version of me?

Could I walk away knowing that I might never receive the acknowledgment or apology I thought I deserved?

And when I saw something genuinely unfair, did I have the courage to say something even when I had nothing personally to gain from it?

Those questions are probably the closest thing this goal will ever have to a measuring system.

There will be no day when I can put a giant check mark next to it and say, completed.

And perhaps that is exactly why I want it on my vision board.

Some of my goals are about what I want to have. Some are about what I want to accomplish. Some are about places I want to reach.

But I want to leave room for goals that are simply about the kind of person I hope to become.

I want to become harder to provoke when the only thing at stake is my ego, and harder to silence when something genuinely matters.

Unlike Half Dome, I don't think there is a summit for this one.

There is only the next moment in which I get to practice.
